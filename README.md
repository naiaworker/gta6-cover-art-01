**Flamingo Bay Cover Studio** turns your own photos into bold, sun-soaked video-game poster covers. Drop in one photo or several, arrange them across a multi-panel layout with white keylines, pick a colour grade (Vice Sunset, Neon Nights, Teal & Orange, Noir B&W), stamp on a big display caption in Anton with poster-style stroke and shadow, and export a finished PNG in one click — at 1080×1080, 1080×1350 or 1920×1080.

- **Seven layouts:** classic 5-panel collage, triptych, hero + strip — plus four new geometries in v2: **Slant duo** (the top pair cut on a diagonal), **Medallion** (a round hero panel ringed by four corners), **Cinematic** (a wide band over a stacked pair), and **Magazine rail** (one tall photo down the side, cover-lines style).
- **Four photo styles:** keep every photo photographic, or treat it — **Game Art** posterizes each photo into flat cel-shaded bands with dark inked edges; **Heatwave** prints it as poster ink, a hot indigo-to-amber duotone under a halftone dot screen with ink outlines; **Dusk Film** lays a warm film fade over it, lifted blacks and fine fixed-pattern grain. Every treatment is deterministic per-pixel arithmetic and combines with any colour grade.
- **Your photos, your call:** drag-and-drop or browse; one photo can fill every panel, or click panels to place photos one by one; drag to pan and scroll or slide to zoom each panel's crop. Crops default to a head-safe focal point, so subjects are not sliced by the frame.
- **Lettering:** editable title and tagline, size and colour controls, top or bottom placement, optional frame line — all rendered into the exported PNG.
- **Private and offline:** plain static files, no libraries, no uploads, no network requests. Photos never leave the machine. Sixteen sample photos are bundled so a first cover takes seconds — ten real coast photographs under the Pexels License (credited in [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md)), five made with an image model, and one very surprised cat.

## Run it

```sh
node serve.ts
```

Then open http://localhost:8080 in a desktop browser. (`node serve.ts --port 8123` serves on a different port.)
